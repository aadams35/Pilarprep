# Stakeholder profiles

## Ariana Cole, Chief Digital Officer
Role type: decision-maker. Organizational role: executive sponsor. Decision authority: strategic priority and final program commitment. Priorities: merchant trust, faster payroll-partner onboarding, and a board-ready risk narrative. Concerns: settlement disruption or a customer-visible incident. Success measure: two design partners complete a bounded first release without replacing the ledger. Engagement guidance: lead with partner growth and customer trust, then connect each technical gate to executive confidence.

## Marcus Vale, Chief Financial Officer
Role type: decision-maker. Organizational role: economic buyer. Decision authority: funding envelope and commercial acceptance criteria. Priorities: shorter onboarding cycles, predictable delivery cost, and measurable partner activation. Concerns: open-ended redesign and duplicated operating costs. Success measure: an investment range tied to onboarding lead time and support effort. Engagement guidance: present decision options and cost boundaries rather than an AWS service tour.

## Dev Malik, VP Infrastructure and Resilience
Role type: decision-maker. Organizational role: technical decision-maker. Decision authority: technical direction, reliability acceptance, and production readiness. Priorities: reuse of the existing AWS platform, idempotent interfaces, failure testing, and named operational owners. Concerns: duplicate instructions, replay failures, unclear MSK ownership, and unsupported RTO/RPO claims. Success measure: a validated interface pattern with failure-mode evidence and approved release gates.

## Rachel Kim, Chief Risk and Compliance Officer
Role type: decision-maker. Organizational role: security and compliance decision-maker. Decision authority: control acceptance, retention decisions, and risk exceptions. Priorities: least privilege, data minimization, attributable privileged access, and audit-ready evidence. Concerns: raw payroll data in logs, broad support access, and inherited retention assumptions. Success measure: approved data classification and retention decisions with control evidence attached to the release gate.

## Priya Shah, Director of Payment Operations
Role type: stakeholder. Organizational role: application and operational owner. Influence: high. Stance: supportive. Priorities: reconciliation accuracy, exception visibility, payroll cutoffs, and unambiguous runbook ownership. Concerns: late acknowledgements, duplicate files, and exception queues without owners. Success measure: exceptions are visible before the next payroll cutoff and daily reconciliation outcomes have an owner. Priya does not hold final funding authority.

## Elena Torres, Payroll Partnerships Lead
Role type: stakeholder. Organizational role: internal champion and partner owner. Influence: high. Stance: champion. Priorities: certification windows, a consistent partner status model, and a repeatable onboarding playbook. Concerns: bespoke partner processes and evidence arriving after certification windows close. Success measure: two design partners complete certification through one governed process.

## Noah Grant, Director of Strategic Procurement
Role type: stakeholder. Organizational role: finance and procurement reviewer. Influence: medium. Stance: neutral. Priorities: commercial clarity, vendor accountability, support boundaries, and predictable renewal impact. Concerns: unplanned tooling commitments and contracts that do not match technical responsibilities. Success measure: commercial dependencies are documented before the pilot commitment. Noah can delay the schedule but does not approve the architecture.

## Omar Fields, Platform Engineering Lead
Role type: stakeholder. Organizational role: technical evaluator and potential blocker. Influence: high. Stance: skeptical. Priorities: low operational burden, reusable interface patterns, clear ownership, and credible on-call capacity. Concerns: a rushed deadline, custom adapters, weak replay tooling, and another service without staffing. Success measure: a supportable reference pattern with capacity, runbooks, test evidence, and explicit ownership. Omar can block implementation readiness but does not own executive funding or risk acceptance.
